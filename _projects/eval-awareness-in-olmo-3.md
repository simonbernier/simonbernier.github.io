> ***Status: in progress.***

I have been quite interested in studying how post-training affects alignment in large language models (LLMs). Most recent training methods nowadays emphasize the use of reinforcement learning (RL) to update model weights such that it takes up about half of the total training compute budget ([cite this,xxx]()). In particular, modern labs use reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to help with the design of reward models based on objective ground truths. Tasks that models are asked to perform include math, where proofs and results are easy to verify numerically or with the use of formal verification programming like Lean. Then, there is substantial efforts being poured into coding tasks, which can also be rigorously checked for correctness with objective tests. RLVR also includes instruction following, where the reward model *rewards* actions that correctly follow a set of instructions likely with tool use on agentic tasks. Finally, the datasets for RLVR usually contain tasks rewarding general capability to perform well in chats.

The main issue with RLVR is that the intermediate steps leading to the final answer, like the content of a model's chain of thought (CoT) or the model's internal activations are very expensive to track and should not be used to modify the training. Tracking the CoT and rewarding the model for "good intermediate thoughts" can teach the model to hide away "bad CoTs", which could create an LLM that is excellent at deception as a byproduct of RL. The same can be said about tracking model internals, the model could learn to modify the computational circuits to make them very hard to interpret for humans, thus making a juge pan of mechanistic interpretability obsolete. Then, if we cannot create reward models for intermediate reasoning steps during RLVR, we cannot be certain of what a model will learn to explore and exploit by itself.

The OLMo 3 family of models is an open language model with various training checkpoints publicly available on HuggingFace. We can track how model capabilities and propensities evolve during training through its various stages, with the datasets also being completely open and freely accessible on the platform ([Olmo Team, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961)). I began by replicating an evaluation awareness study testing vocalized evaluation awareness (VEA) across supervised fine tuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO) and RLVR, choosing about ten intermediate training steps for SFT and RLVR ([Bharadwaj,2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/c2tqL9xPbttisAHtt/tracing-eval-awareness-emergence-through-training-of-olmo-3)), as shown in Fig 1\. There, results show that SFT increase VEA from the base model (which is the model trained up to mid-training), then DPO decreases it after which it is strongly increased through RLVR. This is a different conclusion from what was first reported and a study showed that the model is quite sensitive to system prompt formatting. VEA changes if the system prompt finishes by `<think>` or `<think>\n`, by reflecting DeepSeek completions used during SFT for safety training, where thinking blocks are ended with a newline compared to the data produced by QwenXXX having no newline after the `<think>` tokens ([Lesswrong,2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ivKSFzBZwfadTcEfv/revisiting-verbalized-eval-awareness-in-olmo-and-tracing-it-1)). This can be easily be seen when a model refuses to answer, there will usually be a *certain sentence* present in its CoT. RLVR is then shown to increase the rate at which these sentences occur throughout training. The study shows that rewording the SFT data to remove these DeepSeek phrases and fine-tuning with low-rank adapters decorrelates model refusal from VEA and reduces occurences of VEA. Therefore, my results are not strong indication of evaluation awareness in OLMo 3 and I need to wait for the model to be retrained through SFT and DPO before any RLVR result is deemed conclusive.

For that reason, I looked into OLMo 3.1 ([Team Olmo, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961)), where additional RLVR post-training is done on the same OLMo 3 model. Ai2 also published RL-Zero models, where they post-train with RLVR directly from the base model, completely skipping SFT and DPO. Checking the RLVR Dolci dataset shows that there are little to no occurences of evaluation awareness. Now, because the model does not see the VEA phrases present in the SFT dataset, any increase in evaluation awareness should be attributed to RLVR itself.
